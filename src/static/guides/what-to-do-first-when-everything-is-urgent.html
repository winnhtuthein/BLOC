What to Do First When Everything Feels Urgent

Updated October 19, 2026 · 5 min read

When everything feels urgent, you freeze, or you jump to whatever pinged last. Neither helps. The fix is not working faster. It is deciding what matters, in about five minutes.

Why everything feels urgent

Urgent means it is loud and soon. Important means it has serious consequences. They are not the same thing. A message that just arrived feels urgent, but it may not matter at all by tonight.

The five-minute reset

  1. Write every task down. A list on paper is less scary than a list in your head.
  2. Ask one question about each task: What is the worst realistic outcome if I skip this today? Think about money, trust, deadlines and real problems getting worse. Do not count guilt or habit.
  3. Mark the serious ones. Those are your top tasks. If you have more than three, ask again which three have the worst consequence.
  4. Rank them 1, 2, 3. Start number 1.
  5. Stay on it until it is done. Switching is what makes a busy day feel endless.
  6. Look at the rest. Can anyone else do it? Would anything important happen if you never did it? Hand off or delete what you can.

Two questions that cut through the noise

  • Does it have to be me, or just a person?
  • If I never did this, would anything important happen?

Honest answers to these two often remove a third of the list.

What if two tasks are both serious?

Pick the one with the earlier deadline or the worse consequence. If they truly tie, choose the one that unblocks other work. Then stop deciding and start.

Frequently asked questions

What do I do when everything feels urgent?

Stop and ask one question about each task: what is the worst realistic outcome if I skip it today? Tasks with serious consequences come first, and you pick one and start it.

How many urgent tasks can I really have?

Usually fewer than it feels like. Pick at most three that have serious consequences, rank them, and start the first.
