The ABCDE Method: Rank Your To-Do List in 5 Minutes

Updated October 19, 2026 · 6 min read

Most to-do lists fail for one reason: every item looks equally important. You finish the easy ones, feel busy, and the one task that mattered is still sitting there at 6 pm.

The ABCDE method fixes this with one rule. Before you start, give every task a letter. The method was popularized by Brian Tracy, and it works because the question it asks is simple: what happens if I don't do this?

The five letters

LetterMeaningAsk yourself
AMust do. Serious consequences if skipped.What is the worst realistic outcome if I skip this today?
BShould do. Mild consequences if delayed.Would someone be mildly inconvenienced, or would I pay a small cost?
CNice to do. No real consequence either way.If this never happened, would anyone notice in a week?
DDelegate. Someone else can do it.Does this need my skill, my decision or my presence, or just a person?
EEliminate. Remove it.What happens if I never do this?

How to run it, step by step

  1. Write everything down. Get it all out of your head first. Do not rank yet.
  2. Go through the list once and add a letter to each task. Use the questions above. Be quick; your first honest answer is usually right.
  3. Rank your A tasks. Call them A-1, A-2, A-3. Three at most. If you have six, you have not ranked yet. Ask which one has the worst consequence.
  4. Start A-1 and stay on it. Do not check email, do not switch. Finish it.
  5. Do not touch a B while any A is undone. Then move on to B, then C.
  6. Hand off the D tasks and delete the E tasks. Delegating without a follow-up is not delegating, so note who has what and when you will check.

The three traps

"Everything is an A"

Loud is not the same as serious. A ping is urgent; it is not automatically important. Use the consequence question, not your stress level.

C tasks feel productive

Tidying a desktop is easy, so it feels like progress. It is often procrastination in work clothes, and the first thing people grab to avoid an A.

B tasks quietly become A tasks

A B with a deadline next week is an A in disguise. Give every B a slot on your calendar before it gets there.

A worked example

Say your Tuesday list is: send the client proposal (due tomorrow), book a dentist visit, reorganize your folders, enter receipts into a spreadsheet, and attend a weekly meeting that never has an outcome.

  • Client proposal: A-1. Missing the deadline costs money and trust.
  • Dentist: B. Small cost if it slips, but put it on the calendar.
  • Reorganize folders: C. Only after the A and B are done.
  • Receipts: D. Someone else can do data entry.
  • Pointless meeting: E. Ask whether you would join it if it were new today.

Five tasks, one clear starting point, and two items off your plate.

Frequently asked questions

What is the ABCDE method?

A task-ranking method. You label each task A (must do), B (should do), C (nice to do), D (delegate) or E (eliminate), then work on A tasks first.

How many A tasks should I have?

Three at most, ranked A-1, A-2, A-3. Start A-1 and stay on it until it is done.

Do I need an app for the ABCDE method?

No. Paper works. An app helps once the list is long.
