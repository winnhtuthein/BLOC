Pomodoro Technique: How Long Should a Focus Block Be?

Updated October 19, 2026 · 5 min read

The Pomodoro technique splits work into short, timed blocks with breaks between them. It was created by Francesco Cirillo. The classic version is 25 minutes of work, then a 5 minute break. But 25 minutes is a starting point, not a rule.

The classic version

  1. Choose one task.
  2. Work for 25 minutes. Nothing else.
  3. Take a 5 minute break. Stand up, drink water, look away from the screen.
  4. After four blocks, take a longer break of 15 to 30 minutes.

Picking your own length

LengthGood for
15 minutesStarting a task you are avoiding
25 minutesMost tasks, and a good first try
50 minutesWriting, coding, studying, other deep work
90 minutesOne big job, with a real break after

If you keep wanting to quit at minute 18, shorten the block. If the timer rings just as you get going, lengthen it. Adjust for a week, then keep what worked.

Three rules that make it work

  • One task per block. Pick it before you press start.
  • Breaks are not optional. Skipping them makes the next block worse.
  • If you get interrupted, note it and go on. Write the interruption on paper and deal with it after the block.

What to work on first

Use your first block on your most important task, not on email. See what to do first when everything feels urgent.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a Pomodoro be?

The classic length is 25 minutes of focus followed by a 5 minute break. Many people prefer 50 minutes of focus with a 10 minute break for deep work.

How many breaks should I take?

A short break after every focus block, and a longer one of 15 to 30 minutes after about four blocks.
